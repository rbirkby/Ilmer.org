After the Restoration of the monarchy in 1660, the authorities sought to enforce religious uniformity. The [Act of Uniformity of 1662](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Uniformity_1662) required clergy to accept the prescribed Church of England services and conditions. Ministers who refused lost their posts; many continued preaching to congregations in private houses, barns and other meeting places.

### Answers to Archbishop Sheldon's questions to the clergy concerning non-conformists 1669

source: _Buckinghamshire Dissent and Parish Life, Buckinghamshire Record Society_

> To Mr Richard Claver, Officiall.\
> In answer to your letter, by the commands of his Grace of Canterbury and our Bishop of Lincoln in order to the suppressing of conventicles, I certifye by these presents (haveing considered there Lordships particular directions) that there is one Thomas Saunders, a person that renteth about £20 per annum in this parish of **Ilmer**, at whose house for these two or three yeares past there is each Wednesday in the weeke for the most part a conventicle of about twelve or fourteen persons that goe under the name of Anabaptist. Which are of other parishes and people of ordinary quality whose names I know not nor theire teachers. There numbers hath been observed not much to encrease or decrease and might be, as I suppose, easily suppressed by the assistance of the civill magistrate if the law were duely executed.\
> July 17th 1669 per me Thomas Lillingstone, Curate ibidem

### Bishop Wake's Summary of the responses to his visitation questionnaires from Buckingham Archdeacondy, 1706-12

source: _Buckinghamshire Dissent and Parish Life, Buckinghamshire Record Society_

#### Visitation AD 1706

> (a) Mr Charles Cornish, Vicar.\
> Earle of Carnarvan, Patron.\
> (b) It contains 15 families; of which 2 are non-comformists; one being a Jew, the other a Quaker. No meeting house in the parish.\
> The value of the Vicarege is above £23 per annum.\
> (c) There is neither lecture, school, almes-house, or hospital in this parish.\
> No person of quality or gentleman of estate lives in it.\
> There are no monuments of note in the church nor antiquities in the parish.

#### Visitation AD 1709

> (a) Ordination Gilbert Bristol 29 December 1689.\
> Institution 2 Induction 4 January 1704.\
> Families 13: souls 82; of which 3 dissenters: a Jew and 2 Quakers.\
> (c) No public gift or benefaction, save a parcell of land, value 17 shillings 6 pence given towards the repair of the church.\
> Grosse abuse with relation to the glebe land, to be enquired into.\
> Communicants but 10 at Easter last.

#### Visitation AD 1712

> Families 15: one Quaker: one Jew.\
> The Vicar resides in his Vicarege house.\
> None come to church un-baptized; some not confirmed.\
> Divine service twice every Lord’s Day.\
> Catechizing uncertain; when children come to be examined.\
> Communions 4 times a year; about 7 or 8 receive. Notice duly given.\
> No pennances nor commutations since my last Visitation.
