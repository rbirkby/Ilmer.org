On 23 July 1552, commissioners acting for the government of the young Edward VI recorded[^1] the possessions of St Peter’s Church, Ilmer. The goods were placed in the custody of Edmund Godwyn and William Mede, probably the parish representatives or churchwardens.

[^1]: [Harley MS 1419](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002047249)

The church possessed:

1. Three large bells, a small sanctus bell and a handbell
1. A latten—copper-alloy—processional cross
1. Three copes: one red silk and two described as “changeable colour”
1. Three sets of vestments: red silk, red velvet and changeable-coloured
1. A latten censer for burning incense
1. Four latten candlesticks
1. Three altar cloths and one painted cloth
1. Two linen banners and a cloth for covering the cross
1. Two towels and another linen cloth
1. A partly gilded silver chalice with its paten
1. Two corporals, used beneath the communion vessels
1. A veil cloth, possibly used to cover the rood during Lent
1. Three surplices

The inventory formed part of Edward VI’s [nationwide survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inventory_of_Henry_VIII) of church property during the Reformation. Much of the ceremonial equipment subsequently disappeared as traditional Catholic worship was suppressed.

Transcribed by the Rev J.E. Brown[^2], and published in 1908:

> Item iij awter clothes and a paynted clothe\

> Item ij baner clothes of lynnen and a crosse clothe\

> Item ij corporas and a vayle clothe\
> Item iij surplesys\

> [Three marks]

The names of Edmund Godwyn and Wylliam Mede were written in another hand.
