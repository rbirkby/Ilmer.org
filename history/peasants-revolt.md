Note: The royal pardon for William Matthew of Ilmer connects this event to the peasants revolt.

#### 191. Bledlow, Saturday 31 August 1381.

> **Vills**: Bledlow, Saunderton, 'Great Risborough' and 'Litde Risborough'.\
>
> **Jurors**: Geoffrey Hugendon, John Reynham, John Matthew, Thomas
> Andreu, John Kubbel, Robert Taylour, Robert Priesse, John
> Kughgel, John Semere, John atte Mede, John Coupere and
> John Coltman.
>
> **Verdict**: On Sunday 25 August 1381 William Massy of 'Bristowe',
> Richard Fitzrichard of the same, Robert de Brustowe, William
> Towke of 'Blithe' in Lincolnshire, Richard Fauconer of 'Halys' in
> Staffordshire, and Thomas Merser and William Matthew of Ilmer,
> came by night to Bledlow and feloniously broke into the rectory,
> and entered it. They feloniously killed John Northern, servant of
> Master Roger Otery, parson of the said church, there on the
> Thusday before the said Saturday [sic] with a sword worth 6d., and
> they feloniously stole a missal book, a silver-gilt chalice and other
> ornaments of the said church, and three horses, silver vessels and
> other goods and chattels from the said Rector, 40s. in gold and
> silver from Alexander, parson of Chinnor church, and 30s. from
> Walter, chaplain of Bledlow parish church, who were there at the
> time. They fled at once; no goods or chattels. The jurors also say
> that William Melton of Wycombe, Isabella Hardyconte of Wycombe and Walter Cardemakere of West Wycombe received
> them at Wycombe on the following Sunday, knowing them to be
> felons and to have committed these felonies. Bledlow responsible
> for 6d. deodand.

Massy, FitzRichard, Robert of Bristol, Toukes, Fauconer and Mercer
were outlawed 6 April 1390 (438). William Mathew of Ilmer had a
royal pardon (438) (on 30 January 1382: [C 67/29](https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/r/C2951677), m.16). Richard
Fauconer escaped from gaol and abjured the realm (222); but he was
recaptured, so must have strayed from his route; he appears to have
escaped a second time, and two sheriffs (Thomas Sackville and John
de Aylesbury) were amerced for his escapes ([JUST 3/164](https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/r/C5911209) m. 1). At his
subsequent trial at Aylesbury on Monday 25 May 1383, he pleaded
benefit of clergy and was handed over to the bishop of Lincoln's
ordinary. He had no chattels (JUST 3/164 m.2). Cf CPR 1381-85,
pp.271,481. On 28 July 1391 at Aylesbury Walter Cardemakere of
West Wycombe was acquitted of receiving Massy and his gang after
they had murdered Northern (479). William Melton and his wife
Isabella (Hardyconte), also accused of receiving them, had a pardon
dated 23 May 1382, ratified finally in 1391 for William and 1400 for
Isabella (519). Roger Otery had been presented to Bledlow church in 1344.
At his death, John Cassels was presented on 24 September 1389.

(Lipscomb, II, p. 118).

source: _[Buckinghamshire inquests and indictments](https://bucksrecsoc.org.uk/BRS-VOLUMES/brs-vol-29.pdf#page=162), Bucks Record Society_
